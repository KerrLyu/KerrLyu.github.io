A live classroom cartel game for **ECON 2103: Principles of Microeconomics**. Students join on their phones and are placed in small markets of three or four firms that have agreed to restrict output. Each round, every firm secretly chooses to HOLD (sell one unit) or CHEAT (sell two units), and its profit depends on how many firms in its market cheat. If everyone holds, each firm earns $100; if everyone cheats, each earns only $60.

The game runs in three stages: secret choices with no talking, short meetings before each round with the number of rounds left unannounced, and a final stage in which an antitrust regulator rewards the first firm to report its cartel. The debrief connects students' choices to dominant strategies, Nash equilibrium, repeated play, and leniency policy, using real cases such as OPEC, airlines, lysine, and vitamins.
